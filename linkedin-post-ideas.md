# LinkedIn Post Ideas — GTM Engineer

A running bank of topics + ready-to-post drafts. Pick one, tweak the specifics
to something real that happened to you this week, and post it.

## Content buckets

### 1. Learning in public
- "GTM Engineer Learning Log #1" — a weekly/biweekly recap of one thing you
  built, broke, or figured out. Turns your job search into a visible body of work.
- "3 things I got wrong about cold email before I actually tested them"
- "I spent a weekend automating X — here's what happened"

### 2. Cold email / outbound craft
- Before/after subject line rewrite (show the actual lines, explain the fix)
- "The framework that got me a 40% reply rate" — walk through one real email
  line by line, explain *why* each line exists
- "Stop personalizing with {{first_name}} — here's what actually moves reply rates"
  (talk about trigger-based personalization instead)
- A deliverability post: SPF/DKIM/DMARC, warm-up, sending limits — the
  unglamorous technical side most "growth" people skip

### 3. Tools & stack
- "My GTM stack in 2026" — the tools you actually use (Clay, Instantly/Smartlead,
  Apollo, HeyReach, Trigify, n8n/Make, Airtable) and what each one is *for*
- Tool showdown: "Clay vs. Trigify vs. Apollo for intent signals — when I use which"
- Tutorial-style carousel or thread: "How I built an intent-triggered outbound
  workflow in 20 minutes, no code"

### 4. Trends & hot takes
- "What a GTM Engineer actually does" — clear up the title confusion
  (not SDR, not RevOps, not growth marketing — the overlap of all three)
- "AI agents are taking over SDR busywork. Here's what's still 100% human."
- Contrarian: "Everyone's optimizing for volume. The real unlock is timing."
  (signal-based outbound vs. spray-and-pray)

### 5. Career / job search meta (very on-brand right now)
- "I'm a GTM engineer looking for my next role — so I'm using my own skills to
  find it. Here's the campaign I built." Document your actual job search stack
  and results. This is the highest-leverage post type for you right now: it's
  a live case study of your skills.
- "What I actually look for in a GTM role"

### 6. Engagement bait (use sparingly, keeps the algorithm happy)
- Poll: "Best cold email opener — pick one" (4 options)
- "Drop your worst cold email subject line below, I'll roast it"
- "What's one GTM tool you can't work without?"

## Two ready-to-post drafts

### Draft A — Learning in public

> Spent this week building an intent-triggered outbound workflow instead of
> another spray-and-pray list.
>
> The setup: track a target list for buying signals (job changes, funding,
> LinkedIn activity) → auto-enrich the lead → drop them into a personalized
> sequence within hours of the signal firing.
>
> The unlock isn't "better copy." It's timing. A mediocre email that lands
> the day someone gets promoted beats a great email that lands cold.
>
> Still tuning the trigger thresholds — too sensitive and you're spamming
> noise, too loose and you miss the window. Sharing what I learn as I go.
>
> What signals do you actually act on vs. ignore?

### Draft C — The cold outreach framework (meta, teaches your own method)

> My LinkedIn cold outreach got me 3 interviews a week. Here's the exact
> framework.
>
> Most outreach fails for one reason: we forget we're talking to a stranger.
>
> They don't know you. They don't know if you can actually help them. And
> "I'm interested in this role, here's my resume" doesn't fix that — it just
> adds to the pile.
>
> The only way to stand out is to show them, not tell them. Here's the
> 3-part structure I use:
>
> **1. The hook**
> One line that isn't about you. Something specific to them — a post they
> wrote, a move their company made, a problem people in their seat actually
> have. If it could be copy-pasted to anyone, rewrite it.
>
> **2. Proof + offer**
> Show your research in one sentence, then hand them something useful with
> zero strings attached — a template, a teardown, a quick audit, a resource.
> This is the part that actually builds trust. (You can steal mine — comment
> "template" and I'll send it over.)
>
> **3. The soft ask**
> No "let's hop on a call." No pressure. Just enough of an opening for them
> to say yes easily:
> → "Worth a look?"
> → "Want these?"
> → "Should I send?"
>
> That's it. Hook, value, soft ask. No hard pitch anywhere in it — and
> that's exactly why it works.

Open decisions before posting:
- Turn the CTA into a comment-to-unlock mechanic ("comment 'template' and
  I'll DM it") to drive engagement, or keep it as a pure thought-leadership
  post with no ask?
- Need the actual lead magnet/template written and ready to send the moment
  someone responds?

## How to pick fast when you're stuck

1. Look back at the last thing you built, fixed, or were annoyed by this week.
2. Turn it into one of the 6 buckets above.
3. Write the first draft in your own voice, three sentences max per idea —
   polish later.
